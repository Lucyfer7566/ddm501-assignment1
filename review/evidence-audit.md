# Independent Evidence Audit — `drafts/report-v1.md`

Scope: checked all numbered citations, the report's externally verifiable claims, numerical claims, `research/sources.md`, `research/research-notes.md`, `research/assumptions.md`, and `research/references.bib` against the fixed `scenario.md` and accessible publisher, standards-body, author, and official dataset records. This is an evidence audit of the draft, not a claim that the hypothetical system has been tested.

## CRITICAL Findings

None identified. I found no fabricated citation, dataset, achieved metric, deployment result, or plant-specific baseline in the draft.

## MAJOR Findings

1. **Actual overstatement — unverified plant-specific reason for ML.** `drafts/report-v1.md:31` says the decision *depends on nonlinear, time-dependent relationships* and that a learned classifier can produce a *calibrated risk estimate*. The hypothetical plant has no verified telemetry, timestamped outcome labels, or model validation, so neither property is established for this target. [S04 / Carvalho et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0360835219304838) supports considering ML methods and selecting one for the data and application; it does not establish nonlinear target relationships or calibration for this plant. Revise to a testable hypothesis (for example, the signals **may** contain interactions and temporal patterns that a classifier could use), and state that calibration must be demonstrated by MM-06. The subsequent baseline and promotion gates at lines 33 and 158–164 are sound safeguards, but they do not make the opening assertion factual.

2. **Actual justification gap, not fabricated data — specific acceptance thresholds lack an explicit basis.** `drafts/report-v1.md:131–133`, `:141–158`, and `:240` correctly label the business, service, and model values as **PROPOSED DESIGN TARGETS**; none is falsely presented as achieved. However, the precise choices (for example, 15% downtime reduction, 85% event recall, 50% episode precision, and 1% negative-motor-day FPR) are not derived from an observed baseline, intervention capacity, event prevalence, or cost-of-errors estimate. No S01–S10 source can justify these plant-specific numbers; the source ledger explicitly excludes that use (for example, `research/sources.md:28`, `:79`, `:152`, `:186`). Give the threshold set a short scenario-specific planning rationale and a stakeholder/pilot revision rule, or mark the figures as initial negotiation values to be approved before evaluation. This addresses the assignment's requirement for *justified* thresholds while keeping the fact/assumption boundary intact.

## MINOR Findings

1. **Actual wording ambiguity — MQTT acknowledgement boundary.** `drafts/report-v1.md:182` and `:203` say ingestion durably lands a record *before acknowledgement*, while `:202` uses MQTT and `:221` correctly warns that broker acknowledgement does not prove end-to-end persistence. Under [S07 / MQTT 5.0 §§3.4, 4.3.2](https://docs.oasis-open.org/mqtt/mqtt/v5.0/os/mqtt-v5.0-os.html), a QoS 1 PUBACK acknowledges a PUBLISH at that MQTT hop; it cannot be delayed by a separate downstream ingester to certify object-store landing. Specify that C04 delays **its subscriber acknowledgement to the broker** until durable landing, or introduce a separate application-level receipt for the gateway. Also clarify in SM-04 (`:144`) that gateway/broker acknowledgements are reconciled against downstream persistence rather than treated as proof of it. This is an ambiguity, not evidence that the intended design cannot work.

2. **Actual citation scope issue — idempotency wording is too broad.** `drafts/report-v1.md:221` states that *MQTT delivery semantics require application-level idempotency*. [S07](https://docs.oasis-open.org/mqtt/mqtt/v5.0/os/mqtt-v5.0-os.html) defines three QoS levels; duplication is specifically possible with QoS 1 at-least-once delivery. The design selects at-least-once delivery at `:202`, so change the claim to "the selected at-least-once path requires application-level deduplication/idempotency." This preserves the valid architectural rationale without implying that every MQTT mode itself has that requirement.

3. **Cautious wording suggestion — operational sensitivity is a site policy choice.** `drafts/report-v1.md:109` calls telemetry, predictions, artifacts, and records *operationally sensitive* without a site classification or citation. The controls are sensible as a **DESIGN DECISION**, supported in general by [S08 / NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/82/r3/final), but NIST cannot classify this hypothetical plant's data. "The design treats these data as operationally sensitive, pending site classification" would make the status explicit.

4. **Cautious wording suggestion — model and infrastructure benefits are hypotheses.** `drafts/report-v1.md:265` describes XGBoost as supporting *fast bounded inference* and simpler rollback; `:225` says a full Kubernetes estate and public-cloud dependency are unnecessary. These are plausible design judgments at the stated assumed scale, not measurements or conclusions established by S04. Keep them as **DESIGN DECISIONS** and point to the proposed load, latency, and rollback acceptance tests (`:78–86`, `:162–164`) as the mechanism that will confirm the expected benefits.
